# CROSS-MODAL INTEGRATION (synthesis — where the senses meet)

*Not a new region but a synthesis: the places, all described in their own files, where the
separately-mapped sensory streams **converge and are bound** into unified multimodal representations.
Classical multisensory integration follows a few regularities first established in the superior
colliculus and then confirmed cortically — inputs that coincide in **space and time** combine, often
**superadditively** (the multisensory response exceeds the sum of the unisensory ones), and the
enhancement is largest when each unisensory input is individually weak (**inverse effectiveness**)
(Meredith & Stein; Stein & Meredith — the superior-colliculus rules cited at that entry; Beauchamp
2005 and Calvert et al. 2000 for cortex). This section adds **no new tissue and no new numbers** —
it names no structure whose counts are not recorded where that structure is described.*

***What this section may and may not claim about its own nodes.*** *This preamble used to say that
every node named here "already has its own entry with its own counts." **Checked node by node against
all twenty files on 2026-07-25, that is not true**, and the exceptions are listed with the nodes
below. Two of the nodes named here resolve to **classes rather than regions with entries** — the
temporo-parieto-occipital territory and "the intraparietal multimodal areas" — and one names a
**thalamic division whose parent has an entry and whose division was not confirmed to be named in
it**. A synthesis that overstates the coverage of the map it synthesises is worse than one that
records the gaps, because the overstatement is what stops anyone looking.*

**The convergence nodes, with the state of each in the map:**

- **Superior colliculus, deep layers** — the canonical multisensory integrator: spatially-aligned
  visual, auditory, and somatosensory maps in register with a saccade and orienting motor map, showing
  the superadditive cross-modal enhancement that defines the field (Stein & Meredith). Reflex-speed,
  subcortical, for orienting the eyes and head to salient events. **Has an entry** (file 14), which
  declares the aligned multisensory and motor maps in the same terms and types the outgoing payload as
  a **saccade displacement vector held as a place code** — a location in tissue rather than a value on
  a line. **This is the one convergence node in the file whose output payload is fully typed**, and it
  is worth noting why: the binding is *spatial registration*, and a register is checkable.
- **Superior temporal sulcus and the temporo-parieto-occipital territory** — the cortical
  **audiovisual** convergence zone: it binds a talker's **voice with the face**, integrates auditory
  with visual **speech** (superadditive for speech; Calvert et al. 2000; Beauchamp 2005), and reads
  **biological motion**. It bridges the auditory "what" endpoint with the visual person and face
  stream. **Partly in the map and partly not.** The superior temporal sulcus is covered as part of the
  **anterior superior temporal gyrus and sulcus** entry (file 2), which is the auditory "what"
  endpoint rather than an audiovisual convergence entry. The **temporo-parieto-occipital** territory
  has **no entry**: it is named on an edge in file 6, where it is marked as a class endpoint with its
  payload owed, and it is one of the class endpoints already inventoried in the source's design file.
  **So the file's headline cortical audiovisual node is described here and nowhere else.**
- **Ventral intraparietal area, with the neighbouring intraparietal multimodal areas** — visual motion
  and optic flow, **vestibular**, and facial and head **tactile** signals combined in head- and
  body-centred coordinates: peripersonal and defensive space, with visual and tactile receptive fields
  spatially aligned. **The ventral intraparietal area has an entry** (file 4), which declares the
  vestibular input from the parieto-insular vestibular cortex carrying a computed self-motion
  estimate, and which records an **eye-position or efference-copy signal that no producer supplies** —
  one of the source's unnamed-source items. **"The intraparietal multimodal areas" is a class** and
  resolves to entries in files 4 and 6 only when named.
- **Temporal pole** — the **amodal semantic hub**: highly-processed visual object and face identity
  from inferotemporal cortex, and auditory word and object identity from the anterior superior
  temporal gyrus, bound into **modality-independent concepts** (the hub-and-spoke model), the
  convergence apex of the ventral "what" streams. **Has an entry** (file 7), which records its
  subdivisions and notes that every edge in it belongs to a subdivision and none says which.
- **Claustrum** — the most-connected structure per unit volume, proposed as a cortical **binding and
  synchronisation** coordinator that could unify distributed activity into a single percept
  (Crick–Koch) — a candidate **global** integrator whose role is still debated. **Has an entry** (file
  17), and that entry now records something this section has to carry: **every payload in it is owed,
  including the near-all-to-all cortical output that the binding hypothesis is a claim about.**

**Where the binding claim outruns the payload rule, and this is the section's real finding.** The
source's completion test asks what leaves a region on a wire. At the three highest convergence nodes
named above, the answer the source itself records is that it cannot be written:

- At the **temporal pole**, what leaves is "an amodal concept" — which the source's own design names
  as a phrase rather than a type, alongside the prefrontal cortex's "a goal."
- At the **insula**, the internal edge from the dysgranular middle to the agranular anterior pole
  produces what is described as **subjective feeling**, and that entry marks the payload owed with the
  completion test failing on it explicitly.
- At the **claustrum**, the output edge that all four of its candidate functions are claims about —
  binding, synchronisation, network switching, slow-oscillation control — is owed, and the endpoint is
  a class the source has no convention for.

**These three are not an accident of which files have been worked.** They are the nodes where the
claim *is* that modality-specific formats become modality-independent, and a payload is a statement
about format. So the place where the format is said to dissolve is exactly the place where the payload
cannot be stated. **Whether that is a gap in the literature or a finding about the brain is not
settled here**, and the distinction the source draws between **owed** and **resisted** is the right
instrument for it: owed says nobody has decoded the edge, resisted says it was decoded and yielded no
shared low-dimensional format. **All three currently read owed. Whether any of them should read
resisted is open and unasked.** By contrast the superior colliculus, whose binding is spatial
registration rather than format conversion, types its output payload without difficulty — which is
the contrast that makes the pattern worth recording rather than lamenting.

**Other convergence described in its own file:** the **orbitofrontal cortex** binds taste, smell,
vision, and texture into **flavour** with reward value (files 3 and 11), and its value axis is typed as
**body-relative** — a scalar whose zero and slope move with need; the **insula**, with the pontine
**lateral parabrachial** relay, binds **interoception** with exteroceptive and affective context (files
17 and 14), and the amygdala's contribution to it is typed as a **valence tag** carrying no identity
information; the **hippocampal formation** binds multimodal cortical inputs into **episodes** (file 8),
typed as a **completed episode** that is **content-addressable** — addressable by any fragment of
itself, which is a property rather than a format and is what makes it checkable at all; the
**pulvinar** and **thalamic reticular nucleus** provide transthalamic binding and attentional selection
across visual, parietal, and temporal cortex (file 12); the **magnocellular division of the medial
geniculate nucleus** provides an early auditory-somatosensory convergence — its parent nucleus has an
entry (file 2) and **whether that entry names this division was not confirmed this pass**; and the
medial **default-mode** nodes — posterior cingulate cortex, precuneus, angular gyrus — integrate over
long timescales for self-referential and mnemonic synthesis (files 17 and 6).

**Two convergence nodes the map now describes and this section did not name.** Both come from the
limbic-midline file, and both are cross-modal in the strict sense — they combine signals that arrive
in different formats:

- **Retrosplenial cortex** (file 17) fuses **internal self-motion cues** — vestibular, proprioceptive,
  and motor efference — with **external visual landmarks**, and translates between **egocentric and
  allocentric reference frames**. That frame translation is a conversion between two incommensurable
  formats, which is the strongest form of the problem this section is about. **And the entry records
  that neither half of the fusion is declared at its source**: no incoming edge there carries any of
  the three self-motion cues, and the visual input resolves only to a class.
- **Midcingulate cortex** (file 17) is where **reinforcement outcome meets action**: the anterior
  cingulate cortex supplies a **scalar reward-value signal** on a body-relative axis, the posterior
  cingulate and parietal cortex supply action and spatial information, and the cingulate motor areas
  turn the convergence into a motor plan. **This is convergence across value and space rather than
  across two senses**, which is why it was not in a list built on sensory pairs — and it is the node
  where the source has the most fully typed input payload of any convergence site.

**So cross-modal integration is distributed and hierarchical, not one site:** reflex-speed subcortical
convergence in the superior colliculus and thalamic routing in the pulvinar and the magnocellular
medial geniculate division at the bottom; cortical sensory-pair zones — the superior temporal sulcus,
the ventral intraparietal area — in the middle; amodal conceptual hubs (temporal pole, angular gyrus),
frame translators (retrosplenial cortex), and interoceptive and value integrators (insula,
orbitofrontal cortex, midcingulate cortex) above them; a proposed global coordinator (claustrum)
alongside; and the hippocampal and default-mode systems binding across time. There is no single
"binding site," and no tissue here that is not described where it lives.
